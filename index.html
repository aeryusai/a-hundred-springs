A Hundred Springs

A koi, a crane, and a dream that took a lifetime of petals
Zaragoza, Cyrel

The old crane stood at the edge of the mountain pond, as she had for longer than the village could remember.

Cranes live a thousand years. She remembered every spring.

One morning, a small koi named Kohaku surfaced beside her.

"I'm going to climb the waterfall. The old stories say a koi who reaches the top becomes a dragon."

The crane said nothing. She had heard that promise before.

Each spring, when the sakura petals fell like pink snow on the water, Kohaku tried.

She leapt against the white rush, and the water pushed her back.

Each spring, the crane stood beside her in the evenings, and they watched the petals drift together.

"Why do you stay?"
"I am watching the water."

Years passed.

The village children flew carp streamers over the fields each May, and Kohaku watched them sway — paper koi swimming in the sky, climbing where real koi could not.

She tried again.

She failed again.

Her scales grew dull at the edges.

One evening, the crane finally spoke of long ago.

"There was another koi. Your mother. She tried the waterfall too, one spring. I told her it was impossible."

The crane's voice was quiet.

"She never came back. I have stood here since, in case the water brought her home."

Kohaku said nothing. She just stayed beside the crane until the stars came out.

The next spring, the petals fell.

Kohaku swam to the foot of the falls.

She did not leap with anger.

She climbed — one push, one rest, one push — the way the crane had stood beside her, patiently, year after year.

The waterfall was loud. And then, somehow, it was below her.

Kohaku rose into the morning sky, long and silver, a dragon at last.

She circled the pond once, gently, so the crane would not be startled.

Then she climbed into the clouds.

The crane watched until there was nothing to see.

Now, every spring, when the sakura falls, a silver cloud shaped like a koi passes over the mountain pond.

And the old crane bows her head.

"You climbed. I'm glad I stayed."
Some dreams take a hundred springs.
The water remembers everyone who tries.
